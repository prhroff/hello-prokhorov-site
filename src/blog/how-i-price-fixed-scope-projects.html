I quote most projects at a fixed price. Clients can plan around it, and it keeps me focused on the result rather than the hours. It only works, though, if both of us agree on what's included before anything starts.

What the Scope Says

  • The pages or screens, by name.
  • How many rounds of changes each stage includes.
  • Who writes the copy and who supplies the images.
  • What "done" means: a file, a live site, or a site plus a handover call.

When It Changes

Scope changes are normal; a project teaches you things. When one comes up, I say so as soon as I see it, give the cost in the same message, and let the client choose. Sometimes we swap something out instead of adding to the total.

What I've Learned

The projects that went best were the ones where the first call was spent on scope rather than style. A clear list is less exciting than a moodboard, and far more useful.
