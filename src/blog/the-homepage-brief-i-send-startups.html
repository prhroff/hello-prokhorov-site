Before I design a startup's homepage, I send five questions. They take about an hour to answer, and they save more design rounds than any moodboard.

The Five Questions

  1. Who lands on this page, and what were they doing a minute before?
  2. What do they believe about the problem before they arrive?
  3. What is the one thing they should do next?
  4. What would make them doubt you?
  5. Which sentence about the product would you defend in front of a customer?

Why Plain Sentences

Founders often answer with the language of the pitch deck: platform, seamless, next-generation. I ask them to answer the way they'd explain it to a friend at dinner. That version is almost always closer to the headline we end up with.

What Usually Changes

Question four does the most work. The doubts a visitor has are the sections the page needs: pricing that's unclear, a team nobody has heard of, an integration that might not exist. A homepage that answers those quietly converts better than one that only repeats the promise.

If you're preparing a launch, the brief is part of every project I start.
