The first thing I deliver on a website project isn't a visual. It's a sitemap and a plain-text outline of each page: the sections, in order, with a sentence on what each one has to do.

Why Text First

A visual invites opinions on colour and type. An outline invites the conversation that matters: is this the right order, is anything missing, does this page need to exist at all? Those answers are cheap to change in a list and expensive to change in a finished layout.

What an Outline Looks Like

  • Home — what it is, who it's for, proof, how it works, the next step.
  • Work — three projects, each with the problem and the outcome.
  • Contact — a short form that asks what's needed to reply properly.

Then the Design Is Faster

Once the structure is agreed, the visual work has a clear job. Each section knows what it needs to say, so the design can concentrate on saying it well.
