Prokhorov® — Master Project Brief
=================================

This document is the primary project brief for the Prokhorov® portfolio website.

It defines the purpose, positioning, audience, experience goals, project principles and implementation rules.

It should be read before making major design, UX, motion or technical decisions.

Detailed technical requirements are defined in `TECH.md`.

Creative, UX, motion and technical analysis is defined through the reports in `/agents/reports/`.

* * *

1. Project
   ===========

**Project:** Prokhorov®

**Type:** Personal portfolio website

**Owner:** Artem Prokhorov

**Role:** Independent Designer

**Primary disciplines:**

* UI/UX Design

* Web Design

* Product Design

**Primary domain:**

`helloprokhorov.com`

* * *

2. Project Goal
   ================

Create a distinctive, high-end personal portfolio website that communicates the quality of Artem Prokhorov's design work through the website itself.

The website should not simply display a collection of projects.

It should demonstrate:

* visual taste

* art direction

* product thinking

* web design ability

* typography

* composition

* interaction design

* motion

* attention to detail

* technical execution

The website should feel like a designed digital experience rather than a portfolio template.

* * *

3. Positioning
   ===============

Prokhorov® is the personal portfolio of an **independent designer**.

The website should communicate an individual practitioner rather than an agency, studio or large team.

Use singular language.

The positioning should feel:

* professional

* confident

* independent

* contemporary

* experienced

* approachable

* design-focused

Avoid positioning the business as:

* an agency

* a creative studio

* a large design team

* a generic freelance marketplace profile

* a software development company

* * *

4. Primary Audience
   ====================

The website should primarily work for people looking for a capable independent designer.

Relevant audiences include:

* startup founders

* startup teams

* product teams

* marketing teams

* creative directors

* design leads

* businesses looking for web design

* companies looking for product/UI/UX design

The website does not need to explain design fundamentals to visitors.

The audience should quickly understand:

1. Who is this?

2. What does he do?

3. What kind of work has he done?

4. Is the work relevant to my needs?

5. Does the designer have strong visual and product judgment?

6. How can I start a conversation?

* * *

5. First Impression
   ====================

The first few seconds are critical.

The website should immediately establish:

* identity

* confidence

* visual quality

* personality

* relevance

The first impression should not depend on reading a large amount of copy.

Typography, composition, imagery and motion should communicate the character of the designer immediately.

* * *

6. Experience Goal
   ===================

The experience should feel:

**confident  
editorial  
precise  
premium  
personal  
interactive  
memorable  
controlled**

The website can be experimental.

However, experimentation should always remain intentional.

The experience should never feel:

* random

* chaotic

* gimmicky

* over-engineered

* difficult to navigate

* visually noisy

* * *

7. Creative Ambition
   =====================

The project is allowed to reach a high level of visual and interaction quality comparable to sophisticated award-winning digital portfolios.

Awwwards-level ambition is acceptable as a **quality benchmark**.

This means pursuing:

* strong art direction

* distinctive compositions

* sophisticated typography

* memorable transitions

* carefully designed interactions

* cinematic motion

* high production quality

It does not mean adding effects for the sake of making the website look impressive.

The objective is:

> More creative intelligence, not more effects.

* * *

8. Editorial Direction
   =======================

The website should have a strong editorial character.

Useful principles include:

* large typography

* intentional whitespace

* asymmetry

* visual rhythm

* unexpected alignment

* strong image/text relationships

* changing density

* controlled contrast

* carefully composed sections

The website should feel closer to a high-end digital editorial experience than a conventional portfolio template.

* * *

9. Personal Character
   ======================

The website should communicate that there is a real designer behind the work.

Avoid generic language and generic visual patterns.

The experience should feel authored.

It should have a recognizable point of view.

The website should not look like it could belong to any designer with the name replaced.

* * *

10. Portfolio Philosophy
    ========================

Projects are the core proof of ability.

The portfolio should not treat projects as interchangeable cards in a generic grid.

Project presentation should communicate:

* context

* visual quality

* design decisions

* product thinking

* craft

The layout may vary between projects when this improves storytelling.

Not every project needs to follow exactly the same visual template.

At the same time, the overall website must remain coherent.

* * *

11. Motion Philosophy
    =====================

Motion is part of the design language.

It may be used for:

* establishing hierarchy

* guiding attention

* revealing content

* creating continuity

* transitioning between sections

* connecting visual elements

* creating atmosphere

Motion should feel:

* smooth

* precise

* intentional

* restrained

* cinematic when appropriate

Do not animate every element.

Do not use motion to compensate for weak composition.

The website must remain visually strong even when motion is disabled.

* * *

12. Interaction Philosophy
    ==========================

Interactions should create a sense of depth and responsiveness.

Potential interaction types include:

* hover transformations

* project previews

* image movement

* magnetic interactions

* cursor-based previews

* scroll-linked movement

* pinned sections

* transitions

* progressive reveals

Interactions should have a clear purpose.

They should never prevent users from understanding or navigating the website.

* * *

13. Navigation Philosophy
    =========================

Navigation should remain simple.

Users should always understand:

* where they are

* what content is available

* how to reach projects

* how to contact the designer

Creative navigation is acceptable if it remains intuitive.

Do not sacrifice usability for novelty.

* * *

14. Responsive Philosophy
    =========================

The website should be art-directed across screen sizes.

Mobile is not simply a smaller desktop version.

Desktop and mobile may use different:

* compositions

* image crops

* spacing

* interaction patterns

* motion intensity

* typography scale

* section structures

The underlying visual identity must remain consistent.

* * *

15. Technical Philosophy
    ========================

The website should combine ambitious frontend execution with technical discipline.

The implementation should be:

* performant

* responsive

* accessible

* maintainable

* reliable

See `TECH.md` for detailed technical requirements.

Do not introduce unnecessary technical complexity.

* * *

16. Existing Project First
    ==========================

The existing codebase is the starting point.

Before making major technical decisions:

* inspect the existing architecture

* understand the current implementation

* identify what already works

* identify what should be preserved

* identify what genuinely needs to change

Do not rewrite the project simply because another implementation approach exists.

* * *

17. Decision Making
    ===================

When making a design or technical decision, prioritize:
    1. User experience
    2. Content clarity
    3. Visual hierarchy
    4. Art direction
    5. Interaction quality
    6. Performance
    7. Accessibility
    8. Maintainability
    9. Decorative effects

This hierarchy should guide conflicts between visual ambition, usability and technical implementation.

* * *

18. What Must Be Avoided
    ========================

Do not turn the website into:

* a generic SaaS landing page

* an agency website

* a Webflow-style template

* an AI-generated portfolio

* a crypto/Web3 aesthetic

* a dashboard-like interface

* an over-engineered interactive experiment

* a collection of random visual effects

Avoid:

* unnecessary gradients

* excessive rounded cards

* excessive glassmorphism

* excessive uppercase typography

* unnecessary monospace typography

* generic stock imagery

* repetitive card layouts

* animation everywhere

* meaningless 3D

* unnecessary WebGL

* visual noise

* * *

19. Content Integrity
    =====================

Do not invent project information.

Do not fabricate:

* clients

* results

* statistics

* testimonials

* responsibilities

* awards

* project outcomes

* experience

If information is missing, identify the gap.

Do not fill the gap with invented content.

* * *

20. Design Freedom
    ==================

The project should not be constrained by an overly rigid design system at the beginning.

Agents and implementation should have enough freedom to discover a strong visual solution.

Existing preferences and principles are constraints.

They are not a complete visual specification.

The final design should emerge from:
    Project goals
    +
    Portfolio content
    +
    Creative direction
    +
    UX direction
    +
    Motion direction
    +
    Technical feasibility

* * *

21. Agent System
    ================

The project uses five specialized agents.
    01 Creative Director
            ↓
    02 UX & Interaction Director
            ↓
    03 Motion & Interaction Designer
            ↓
    04 Frontend & Performance Engineer
            ↓
    Implementation
            ↓
    05 Final Design Critic & Creative QA

Each agent has a specific responsibility.

Agents should not duplicate each other's work unnecessarily.

* * *

24. Implementation Principle
    ============================

Do not begin implementation simply because a visual idea sounds interesting.

Every major change should answer:

* Why does this improve the experience?

* What does it communicate?

* Does it strengthen the portfolio?

* Does it fit the overall direction?

* Is it technically reasonable?

* Does it work responsively?

If the answer is unclear, reconsider the change.

* * *

25. Definition of Success
    =========================

The redesign succeeds when the website feels like a complete authored experience rather than a collection of design techniques.

A visitor should leave with a clear understanding that:

**Prokhorov® is an independent designer with strong visual taste, product thinking and the ability to create high-quality digital experiences.**

The website should communicate this through its own execution.

* * *

26. Final Quality Bar
    =====================

Before launch, the website should feel:

**distinctive  
intentional  
personal  
confident  
editorial  
interactive  
precise  
memorable  
fast  
reliable**

The final result should not feel like it is trying to impress the visitor.

It should feel like the quality of the work is simply evident.

The website itself is the strongest case study.
