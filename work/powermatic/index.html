(02)Challenge

A technology partner has to show how it works before anyone gets in touch.

The site speaks to growing companies that need a team for their product. People in that position usually compare several partners at once, and they want to know early how an engagement works and roughly what it costs.

So the first screen had to carry the offer, the way of working and a starting price. The site also has a second audience, people who might join the company, and they needed a way in of their own.

(03)Approach

Each page opens with what its visitor came for.

I put the facts a buyer checks first into the hero row of the home page. A visitor sees the engagement model, the starting price, the process and support in one screen, and can tell whether Powermatic fits their budget before scrolling to the cases.

(In Practice)

  • The engagement model and starting price in the hero row
  • Careers opens straight on the open roles, in large type
  • The menu opens on the section you are reading

(04)Visual Direction

Four columns, drawn on the page.

The grid is visible. Four columns run as thin lines with small crosses where they meet, and every block sits on them, from the hero to the footer. Headings are trimmed to cap height, so their tops meet the lines exactly.

The logo pairs a mark of two chevrons with the wordmark. The palette, Graphite, keeps to near-black and off-white and never uses pure black or white, so long pages stay easy on the eyes. Photos are monochrome with a light grain.

For texture I set one ASCII drawing in Geist Mono. It appears four times on the home page in different tones and angles, which gives an engineering company a look of its own without illustrations.

(05)Key Pages

The home page leads with the offer and the price, so a buyer can rule Powermatic in or out on the first screen.

From the hero, the home page moves through cases, approach, pricing, the blog and questions to the contact form. The menu holds nine links on a drum that turns with the wheel, a drag or the arrow keys.

Careers has its own path. A team filter folds the other rows away, and on a role page the salary range stays in a sticky card beside the application form.

(Decision)

Considered
Careers on the home page layout, with a hero and blocks below it. My first version did this, and it read as the same page again.
Chose
A composition of its own: a thin bar instead of a hero, the open roles as the first screen, a label column on the left and the content on the right.
Because
A candidate sees every role with its team, location and salary without scrolling, while the page keeps the home page’s type, grid, menu and footer.

(06)Motion & Build

The logo fills as the page actually loads.

The preloader fills the logo with the page’s real loading progress, fonts and the hero photo included, then lifts away to open the hero. On scroll, headings rise line by line and images open with a wipe. The ASCII drawings decode into place, and the About text lights up word by word.

In the footer the logo assembles over a lattice of dots that follows the cursor. With reduced motion turned on, the page stays still.

I built the site in HTML, CSS and JavaScript without a framework. A small Python script generates Careers, About and the blog from one shared shell, so a change to the menu or footer reaches every page.

(Built With)

  • HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework
  • Lenis for smooth scrolling
  • A Python script for the Careers, About and Blog pages
  • Job posting and article markup for search
  • An application form with CV upload by drag and drop
